Marginalia

Every line that stopped me, for over a decade, made askable.

Marginalia are the marks readers leave in the edges of a book: the underlines, the stars, the arguments scribbled back at the author. Since 2014 my Kindle has been keeping mine: philosophy, sailing, startups, attention, Africa and its diaspora, utopias, how to live.

Ask it something. It finds the passages that answer back, the books they came from, and (when a model is connected) writes a short reply built only from those lines.

How it works

Four steps, no magic.

  1. Collect

    Every highlight and note from my Kindle's My Clippings.txt, de-duplicated (Kindle re-saves a line each time you extend it) and cleaned up.

  2. Retrieve

    Your question is matched against every passage with BM25, the classic search ranking. Fast, free, and it runs on this site's own small server.

  3. Recommend

    Passages are grouped by book, so the books that keep answering your question rise to the top of the shelf.

  4. Reply

    The best passages go to a language model with one rule: answer only from these lines, and cite them. The model is pluggable.
